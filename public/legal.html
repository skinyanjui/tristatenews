3. Police & courts items: presumption of innocence

The Police & courts section shows items from official public sources only: police and sheriff’s offices, prosecutors, U.S. Attorneys’ offices, the FBI and courts. We show the agency’s headline and a short excerpt with a link to the original, never a photo, and we leave out items about juveniles. An arrest or charge is only an accusation. Everyone charged with a crime is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in a court of law. Items that describe an arrest or charge carry a “Charged or arrested, not convicted” note. These arrest and charge items are removed automatically after 14 days, whichever office published them (police, sheriff, prosecutor, U.S. Attorney, FBI or court). Releases about how a case ended, such as a guilty plea, conviction, sentencing or acquittal, and other announcements stay for up to 30 to 90 days, depending on the source. Cases are often dismissed, reduced or resolved after an item was published, and we don’t track outcomes. If an item is about you, see our Corrections & Removals policy.

Email unclesamjunkremoval@gmail.com, or use Submit news and choose “Correction.” Include the link to the item on Tri-State News, what’s wrong or why it should come down, and any documents that help (for example a court order or a corrected story). We usually reply within two business days.

2. Stories from other publishers

  • We show headlines and excerpts as the publisher wrote them. If the publisher corrects or updates the story, our copy usually updates on the next refresh, which happens every 10 to 30 minutes.
  • If the original is wrong, please also contact the publisher; we’ll tell you who it is.
  • If a publisher deletes a story, removes a name or issues a correction, we’ll remove or update our item on request.
  • We’ll also remove an item that is clearly wrong, shown under the wrong town or section, or that a publisher asks us to remove.

3. Police & courts items

  • We show only items published by official sources, with no photos and nothing about juveniles. Items that describe an arrest or charge carry a “Charged or arrested, not convicted” note. Arrest and charge items come down automatically after 14 days, from every kind of source. Items about a plea, conviction, sentencing or acquittal, and other releases, come down after 30 to 90 days, depending on the source.

In Indiana, expungement is governed by Indiana Code chapter 35-38-9; Kentucky and Illinois have their own procedures.
  • Crime victims, minors and people in protective programs can ask us to remove an item that identifies them. We’ll act quickly and don’t need a reason.
  • We can’t change what the agency or court published; please ask them as well.

Family members and funeral homes can ask us to correct or remove any obituary listing. We’ll do it without asking for proof beyond what’s reasonable. Obituary listings from papers and funeral homes leave the site after 21 days, and obituaries posted on Tri-State News after 30 days.

Effective October 17, 2026 · Last updated October 18, 2026

The short version. Ads and paid placements on Tri-State News are always labeled, never disguised as news, never targeted at you, and kept off news, Police & courts and obituaries. Advertisers have no say in which stories we show.

1. Kinds of paid placement

  • Sponsor lines: a single-line sponsor message at the top of a board, labeled “Sponsored.”
  • Sponsored cards: one card inside a board’s list of posts, labeled “Sponsored”: after the sixth post, or after the last post on a shorter board, and never on an empty board. You’ll see at most one per board, on phones and computers alike.
  • Side ads: ads in the side margins on wide screens, labeled “Sponsored.”
  • Our own notices: when one of these spots hasn’t been sold, it shows our own notice, labeled “Advertise with us,” which opens our advertising inquiry form. It isn’t an ad for anyone else.
  • Placeholder sample ads: for now, some unsold spots show placeholder sample ads labeled “Sample ad” instead, to show what an ad looks like. The businesses in them are made up, not real advertisers, and each one links only to our advertising page. A real, approved ad always takes the spot first, and samples follow the same rules as real ads: only on the four boards, never on News, All, Police & courts, Obituaries or breaking-news alerts.
  • Featured posts: a job, deal, event or classified post whose poster paid to keep it at the top of its board, with a “Featured · Paid” badge. Prices, periods and refunds are in our Terms of Use.
  • Advertising packages: available by inquiry. Their contents are still being finalized, so ask us for current details.
  • Newsletter sponsorship: not offered yet. We’ll offer it only once the newsletter has enough confirmed subscribers, we’ve measured that it reaches inboxes reliably, and we’ve set how many sponsor spots each issue carries. When it’s offered, each sponsor message will be labeled “Sponsored.”

An ad is words alone, or one still image with a short headline and text. We don’t use animation, autoplaying video or sound, pop-ups, or ads that stick to the screen or cover what you’re reading.

2. Where ads appear

  • Sponsor lines, sponsored cards and side ads appear only on the Events, Jobs, Deals and Classifieds boards. Side ads show only on wide screens.
  • There are no ads on News, All, Police & courts, Institutions, Obituaries, breaking-news alerts or search results, and we don’t place ads next to stories about crimes, deaths or other tragedies.
  • The one exception on Obituaries is the “Send flowers” link described in section 8. It is a small text link only, not an ad.

3. No targeting

Our own ads are never targeted. Every reader of a board sees the same ad; when a board has more than one sponsor, they take turns by the date. We don’t use your location, the stories you read, cookies or any other information about you to choose an ad, and we don’t record who sees or clicks an ad. If we ever add an ad network (see section 9), its ads may be personalized only under that network’s own rules and our updated policies.

4. Separation from news

  • Which stories appear, and in what order, is decided automatically by date, place and topic. Advertisers can’t pay to add, remove, move or change a news item, an obituary or a Police & courts item.
  • Obituaries are always free to post, and there is no paid featuring for them.

5. Labels and links

Every ad and paid placement is labeled (“Sponsored” or “Featured · Paid”), as the FTC requires, our own notices are labeled “Advertise with us,” and placeholder sample ads are labeled “Sample ad.” An ad’s link goes to a secure (https) web page, a map or directions page, or a phone number to call, and is marked rel="sponsored" for search engines; web links open in a new tab. Clicking one takes you to the advertiser’s site (or the map service), which has its own terms and privacy practices.

6. What we accept

We accept ads from local businesses, organizations and events that are lawful, truthful and not misleading, and that follow the FTC’s advertising rules. We don’t accept ads for adult content, illegal products, weapons, vaping or tobacco, payday or predatory loans, gambling where it’s illegal, “miracle” health claims, or political ads that don’t identify who paid for them. We can decline or remove any ad. Advertisers are responsible for their ad’s content and any claims in it.

7. Ad images, words and review

Last updated October 18, 2026

Advertisers send their ad through the upload link we give them: it needs the reference from their advertising inquiry (with the email used on it; an inquiry we’ve marked as spam doesn’t count), their order number, or a signed link from us that works for 14 days. Nobody can upload an ad anonymously.

  • Image: one still JPEG, PNG or WebP image, at least 300×150 pixels, no more than 4,000 pixels on a side, and 300 KB or smaller. No animation, video or sound. It must be legible and not imitate a news story, a Tri-State News label, a button that does something other than open the ad, or a system message.
  • Words: a headline of up to 60 characters (also used as the image’s description for screen readers) and optional text of up to 140 characters. No web addresses in the words.
  • Button: one of “Visit website,” “Shop now,” “Learn more,” “Call now” or “Get directions.”
  • Destination: a secure (https) web page, a U.S. phone number for “Call now,” or a Google Maps, Apple Maps, Bing Maps or OpenStreetMap link for “Get directions.” It must be the final address, not a link shortener or a redirect to another site, and must not ask for a password or install anything. The landing page must match the ad.
  • Content: everything in section 6 applies. Claims must be truthful and supportable, and the advertiser must have the rights to every image, logo and word in the ad.

Review. A person at Tri-State News reviews every ad before it runs; nothing uploaded appears on the site until it’s approved. We approve or reject each ad with a recorded reason. An approved ad shows only in the spots we chose for it (sponsor line, sponsored card or side ad). We can take an approved ad down at any time, for example if its page changes or it breaks these rules, and we record why. Rejected and withdrawn images are deleted; we keep a short record of the decision.

8. Affiliate links

Some links on the site may be affiliate links: if you buy something after following one, we may earn a commission, at no extra cost to you. Each affiliate link is labeled “paid link” and marked rel="sponsored". The only place we may use one is the “Send flowers” link below, and only once we’ve joined a flower-delivery affiliate program; until then no affiliate links appear.

“Send flowers” on obituaries. An obituary may show a small “Send flowers” text link to a florist. Where we’ve confirmed that the funeral home has its own florist page, the link goes there. Otherwise it goes to an online flower-delivery service. When the link is marked “paid link,” it’s an affiliate link: we may earn a commission on an order, at no extra cost to you. A “Send flowers” link without that label earns us nothing. It is a link only: no ad, image, script, cookie or tracking pixel is added to the obituary, and the link carries nothing about you or the person who died. A commission never affects which obituaries appear or what they say. We’ll name the service here when we join its program.

9. Ad networks

We don’t use any third-party ad network today. If we ever show ads from a network such as Google AdSense, we’ll update this policy and our Privacy Policy, and show a consent notice where the law requires one, before any network ad appears. Network ads will be labeled “Advertisement” and kept out of the sections listed in section 2.

10. No endorsement

An ad isn’t a recommendation from Tri-State News. If we ever write about an advertiser, we’ll disclose the relationship.

Accessibility Statement

Effective October 17, 2026 · Last updated October 17, 2026

In this section
  • 1. Our goal
  • 2. Known limitations
  • 3. Tell us about a problem
  • 4. How we check

We want everyone to be able to read local news on Tri-State News, including people who use screen readers, keyboards, screen magnifiers or voice control.

1. Our goal

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, level AA. What we do so far:

  • A “Skip to stories” link, real headings and labeled page regions.
  • Every control works with a keyboard and shows a visible focus outline. Buttons and links are at least 40 pixels in size where possible.
  • Buttons such as the heart and share icons have text labels for screen readers, and the heart announces whether it’s on or off.
  • Colors are chosen for readable contrast in both light and dark themes. The site follows your system’s dark-mode setting, or you can choose with the theme button.
  • Pages adapt to small screens and to browser zoom.
  • Form fields have labels, and errors are announced and explained in words.

2. Known limitations

  • Stories, images and documents on other publishers’ sites are outside our control, and some may not be accessible.
  • Thumbnails come from publishers and are marked as decorative, because we don’t have descriptions for them. The headline next to each one tells you the story.
  • Photos on the community boards come from the people who post them, and posters can’t add descriptions yet.

3. Tell us about a problem

If something on Tri-State News is hard to use, email unclesamjunkremoval@gmail.com with the page and what happened. We’ll try to reply within five business days, and we can send you the information you need in another format.

4. How we check

We review accessibility with keyboard-only navigation, screen readers and automated tools when we change the design. This statement was last reviewed on October 17, 2026.
